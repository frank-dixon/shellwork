Color

Egg + hen, paired

Shell color comes from the hen, not from what she ate that week. Search or filter by egg-color family, then pick a breed to see her traits beside the egg she is known for — white, tinted, brown, dark chocolate, blue, or olive.

Brown shells wear protoporphyrin IX deposited late in the shell gland onto the outer shell and cuticle. Blue-green shells carry biliverdin (oocyan) earlier in the matrix, so the tint shows through the full thickness. Layer brown over blue and you get olive. White eggs simply lack those pigments — calcium carbonate shows through. Breed notes cite Koops Coops, Murray McMurray Hatchery, and Cackle Hatchery (Gail Damerow).

Shape

Round, classic, pointed

Most table eggs are gently oval: a blunt end and a slightly sharper tip. Breed and hen age nudge that silhouette. Sudden runs of very odd shapes can flag stress or health issues; everyday scatter is normal flock variation.

Shape index (width ÷ length × 100) near the mid-70s describes a classic table egg; lower numbers read more elongated. Membranes and the shell gland set the form hours before lay. Light Mediterranean birds can look a touch more elongated; heavy dual-purpose hens often look rounder. Extreme misshapes deserve a flock health check.

Leghorn cue

Sleek, upright body; eggs often a clean classic oval rather than a squat sphere.

Orpington cue

Broad, low posture; shells can read a touch rounder beside the nest straw.

Rhode Island Red cue

Medium dual-purpose frame; brown eggs usually sit in the everyday oval band.

Size

Weight classes, breed norms

Carton names are weight per dozen. A Large egg is about two ounces. Light Mediterranean breeds such as White Leghorns often sit Medium–Large; heavier dual-purpose hens like Orpingtons and mature Rhode Island Reds push Large and Extra Large as they age.

USDA size names are minimum net weights per dozen, not genetic labels. Egg weight rises with hen age and body weight and responds to nutrition and day length. Breed sets a typical band — Leghorns stay relatively light; Orpingtons and other heavy dual-purpose birds trend heavier.

White Leghorn

Compact flighty layer; eggs often Medium–Large with thin white shells.

Rhode Island Red

Medium dual-purpose frame; mature hens commonly Large brown eggs.

Buff Orpington

Heavy, broad body; tinted to light-brown eggs often Large–XL once mature.
